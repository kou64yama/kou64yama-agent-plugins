# Accessible Technical Writing

Apply accessibility throughout drafting and review, not as a final cosmetic
pass. Follow applicable standards for the medium and verify requirements when
they matter.

## Review workflow

1. Identify the formats and interaction modes readers use, including assistive
   technology, keyboard navigation, zoom, and nonvisual access.
2. Check headings for a meaningful hierarchy; use descriptive link text and
   clear labels. Avoid relying on visual position or formatting alone to convey
   structure or instructions.
3. For each image, decide whether it is informative or decorative. Write concise
   alt text that conveys the image's purpose in context; use empty alt text for
   decoration. For complex diagrams, explain the important relationships in
   surrounding text or a longer description.
4. Check that meaning is not conveyed by color alone. Pair color with labels,
   shapes, patterns, or text; ensure contrast and legibility in the actual
   medium.
5. Use respectful, inclusive language. Prefer the terminology used by the people
   concerned, avoid unnecessary assumptions about identity or ability, and
   distinguish a person's needs from a product or environment's barriers.
6. Review the content in its rendered form and with the relevant accessibility
   checks. Fix barriers at their source and preserve equivalent information
   across formats.
